# Which name does a gate print when the NWT disagrees with everyone else?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

The NWT name research found the game will teach names that many speakers of the same language never say. Georgian is the worst case — NWT `კანონი` for Deuteronomy against `მეორე რჯული` in every other Georgian Bible — then Russian (6 hard conflicts plus a dropped preposition across all 21 New Testament books), then Dutch (`Rechters` vs `Richteren`).

Book Order is gate *choice*, so "accepting an alias" is not a feature: there is nothing to accept. The decision is **what a gate prints**, and that is what the player learns whether they win or lose the run.

**Settled by the operator: the NWT naming, exactly.** Gates print the NWT name; section and course labels use the NWT's own wording (`HEBREW-ARAMAIC SCRIPTURES`, `CHRISTIAN GREEK SCRIPTURES`) rather than Old/New Testament. That implies the audience is JW readers of the NWT, not a general Bible audience — so NWT spelling is the truth the game teaches, and the common name is at most a courtesy.

**Settled by the operator: NWT names only, ever.** The common name appears nowhere — not on the gate, not on the reveal card, not on the review. One vocabulary, the one the audience actually reads.

Judge it by the failure mode: a Georgian or Russian player who learned `მეორე რჯული` / `1 Царств` sees `კანონი` / `1 Самуила` and must not read it as a different book or a bug. English has zero conflicts, so any alias display there is pure noise; Russian and Georgian have hard conflicts on 6 and ~29 books and need the courtesy if anyone does.

The same question applies to the labels themselves, not just book names. The NWT's own divisions are `HEBREW-ARAMAIC SCRIPTURES` / `CHRISTIAN GREEK SCRIPTURES` (`ЕВРЕЙСКО-АРАМЕЙСКИЕ ПИСАНИЯ` / `ХРИСТИАНСКИЕ ГРЕЧЕСКИЕ ПИСАНИЯ`, `Boeken van de Hebreeuwse Geschriften` / `Griekse Geschriften`), where most Bibles in these languages print an equivalent of Old/New Testament. Two of the five courses are named after those NWT divisions, so decide whether course and section labels follow the NWT's wording or the common wording — and remember the research's sourcing rule applies: derive from public-domain texts, cross-check against the NWT.

Answer alongside it: **who is this for?** A JW audience that reads the NWT, or a general Bible audience where NWT naming would be wrong more often than right. That answer decides whether this is a per-locale policy or a single one.

## Answer

**NWT naming exactly, and only.** Gates print the NWT book name; section and course headings use the NWT's own labels; no common name, alternate spelling, or "also called" line appears anywhere — gate, reveal card, end-of-run review, or scoreboard. The game teaches one vocabulary: the audience's own.

**What this decides for other tickets:**

- The content table carries a single canonical name per book per locale. **No alias column.** The research's alias tables are unused.
- The research's biggest uncertainty disappears with it: the Georgian *common* names were secondary-sourced and flagged as needing a native speaker. The **NWT** Georgian names came from a primary source (WOL), so dropped aliases remove the project's weakest data.
- Section and course labels follow NWT wording (`HEBREW-ARAMAIC SCRIPTURES`, `CHRISTIAN GREEK SCRIPTURES`, `ЕВРЕЙСКО-АРАМЕЙСКИЕ ПИСАНИЯ`, `Boeken van de Hebreeuwse Geschriften`, and their Georgian forms) rather than Old/New Testament.
- Still subject to the sourcing rule from the names research: order and structure from a public-domain Bible, NWT spelling cross-checked manually, provenance recorded, no scripture text.

**Accepted risk:** a Russian or Georgian player who learned `1 Царств` or `მეორე რჯული` meets `1 Самуила` or `კანონი` with no explanation anywhere in the game. The reveal card after a bounce shows the correct NWT name and nothing else. That is a deliberate choice to teach one vocabulary rather than two, not an oversight.
